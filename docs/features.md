# What is in Vantage

Every feature, where it is in the app and the figures measured with it. The step-by-step guide with a worked example for each is [user-guide.md](user-guide.md); the models behind them are in [overview.md](overview.md).

| Area | Features |
|---|---|
| Vehicle library | 13 real vehicles with their makers' stated dimensions and sources: Renault ZOE (nuScenes), VW Passat B6 Variant (KITTI), Lincoln MKZ 2017, Toyota Prius XW50 and Sienna, Chrysler Pacifica Hybrid, Hyundai IONIQ 5, Kia e-Niro, Lexus RX 450h, Tesla Model 3, Mercedes-Benz Sprinter 170″ high roof, Tata Nexon.ev, and a Freightliner Cascadia 126 day cab with a Wabash 53 ft van. Length, width with and without mirrors, height, wheelbase, track, overhangs, ground clearance, tyre and kerb mass. A template body lofts the vehicle's family profile to the stated figures, shaped with glass, alloy rims, lamps, black trim and mirror pods. A figure not stated is drawn and named. View ▸ Overlays ▸ Range rings (Alt+R) marks 1, 2, 5 and 10 m from the body on the ground. The MKZ (Dataspeed, BSD 3-Clause), Prius (Open Robotics, CC0) and RX 450h (Autoware, Apache 2.0) 3D models download from their publishers on request (Vantage asks first) and open in the import wizard with the stated figures for the spec check; none ships with Vantage. |
| Vehicle import (Ctrl+I) | .glb .gltf .obj .stl .ply .dae (COLLADA) .fbx (binary and ASCII), .step via the optional gmsh package. Wizard: units (guessed from the bounding box), up/forward axes and mirroring, inside-out meshes flipped automatically, wheel centres (auto from wheel parts or picked on the mesh), ground plane, origin preset, spec check against datasheet dimensions, occluder simplification, mount zones painted with a brush, glass detected from materials and transparency. |
| Rig (Vehicle ▸ Add rig part) | The data-collection rig on the vehicle as simple solids: **roof rack** (two crossbars and a platform), **sensor mast** (an upright tube with a head 0.5 m above the roof), **sensor enclosure** and **crossbar**, each sized and placed from the vehicle's roof and added as one undo step. Parts are boxes, upright cylinders or bars across the vehicle (centre, size, yaw). A part that *occludes* blocks cameras exactly like the body (ground coverage, blind volume, camera images, the installation check's synthetic captures, the assistant's *why is this point not seen*, which names the part); a *mountable* one carries cameras (place on it, drag along it with surface snapping; a camera on a part that is not mountable is flagged as on a forbidden zone). Listed in the Outliner (select, hide, delete), edited in the Inspector, drawn in 3D and as outlines in the top view, picked with a click; saved with the project and exported as fixed links with box/cylinder geometry in the ROS 2 URDF, a `rig` list in the extrinsics and a table in the report. |
| Lidars, radars, GNSS and IMU (Vehicle ▸ Add lidar / Add radar / Add GNSS antenna / Add IMU) | Lidars and radars on the rig next to the cameras, per variant. The library holds real products only, with the figures their makers state in public datasheets, manuals and product pages (read 2026-11-04; each model names its source and what its maker does not state). **Lidars:** Ouster OS0-128, OS1-128 (Rev 7, ±21.2°, 90 m on a 10 % target) and OS2-128; Hesai OT128, AT128 and XT32; Velodyne VLP-16, HDL-32E (nuScenes' lidar) and HDL-64E S2/S3 (KITTI's); RoboSense RS-Ruby Plus; Livox Mid-360 and HAP (non-repetitive, modelled as an even grid with the same points per frame). **Radars:** Continental ARS 408-21 (nuScenes' radar), ARS 548 RDI and SRR 308-21; smartmicro DRVEGRD 152, 169 and 171 (4D). **GNSS antennas:** NovAtel VEXXIS GNSS-850, u-blox ANN-MB-00. **GNSS/INS and IMUs:** OxTS RT3000 v4, NovAtel PwrPak7D-E1 and SPAN CPT7, SBG Ellipse-D and Ekinox-D, Xsens MTi-630, Bosch BMI088. Projects that used the generic placeholder models of earlier versions move to the real model that replaced each, and the design log says so. A lidar goes on top of the highest mountable rig part (a mast head) or the roof, a radar in the middle of the front bumper; then move it in the Inspector (optical centre, yaw/pitch/roll like a camera), drag it in the top view, or pick another model. A lidar is a channel fan (beam elevations, azimuth step, frame rate, range on a 10 % target, points per second, bytes per point, spin direction); a radar is one or more zones (range on a car, ±azimuth, ±elevation), smaller targets reaching the fourth root of their radar cross-section. Editing a built-in model's figures makes an edited copy for that sensor; **Import beam angles…** reads the measured channel elevations from an Ouster metadata JSON, a Hesai angle-correction CSV, a Velodyne ROS calibration YAML or a plain list. Drawn in 3D (housing; the lidar's ground rings, where its downward beams meet the ground; the radar's field edges) and in the top view (rings, radar fields, footprints, tags), picked with a click, listed in the Outliner (select, hide, delete). The Issues list flags a lidar inside the body or a rig part, a radar deeper than 15 cm behind the bumper surface, a sensor below the ground and a tag a camera also uses. Saved with the variant; exported as frames (x forward, y left, z up at the optical centre) in the extrinsics (with the field and spin), the ROS 2 URDF (links with the housing) and static TF launch file, the nuScenes `sensor` / `calibrated_sensor` tables (`LIDAR_<TAG>`, `RADAR_<TAG>`), KITTI (`Tr_<tag>_to_vehicle`, and `calib_velo_to_cam.txt` for the first lidar into camera 00) and the report. **GNSS antennas and IMUs** sit on the rig the same way: an antenna's position is its phase centre (a typical survey antenna and a compact patch antenna in the library), an IMU's the centre of its axes and its frame its own axes (typical dual-antenna MEMS GNSS/INS, FOG GNSS/INS and MEMS IMU classes, with heading error at a stated baseline, RTK position error, roll and pitch error, output rate, antenna inputs, and the accelerometer and gyro noise densities and bias random walks that Kalibr's `imu.yaml` takes; not products: enter your unit's figures). The first antenna goes near the rear edge of the roof (or the rack over it), the second near the front edge for the longest heading baseline, the IMU on the cabin floor above the rear axle; the Inspector shows each antenna's baseline and the INS's heading error for it, the 3D and top views draw the baseline and the IMU's axes. The Issues list flags an antenna inside the body or a rig part (no sky); an IMU belongs inside. They are exported in the extrinsics, the URDF and TF, KITTI (`Tr_<tag>_to_vehicle`, and `calib_imu_to_velo.txt` from the first IMU into the first lidar) and the report; nuScenes has no GNSS or IMU channels (they make its `ego_pose`). The recording budget counts an INS's output. |
| Camera modules | Built-in library of 36 real camera modules and 52 lenses. **e-con Systems** (22): the enclosed STURDeCAM automotive cameras (STURDeCAM20, 21, 25, 29, 31, 31FSH with heated fisheye lens, 34, 39, 57, 81, 84, 88), the NileCAM21, 56, 81, 85, 130 and 210 GMSL2 board cameras, and the FPD-Link NeduCAM25, NeduCAM81, Nedu4CAM57 and Nedu4CAM88. **Other makers** (14): Leopard Imaging LI-IMX390, LI-IMX490 and LI-IMX728, D3 Embedded Discovery PRO IMX390 and IMX728, Sekonix SF3324/SF3325 (NVIDIA DRIVE), the public datasets' cameras (nuScenes' Basler acA1600-60gc with its 5.5 mm lens, KITTI's FLIR Flea2 colour and greyscale with their 4 mm lens), Teledyne FLIR Blackfly S and LUCID Triton. Each carries the figures its maker's pages state (sensor, resolution, pixel pitch, frame rate, shutter, dynamic range, IP rating, operating temperature, power, size, cables), the page and the date it was read, and a list of the figures it does not give (never filled in: a camera without a stated power counts 0 W in the power budget and is listed). Each lens reproduces its maker's stated field of view within 0.6°; a stated focal length and aperture are kept, and where the maker states none (e-con) the focal length is derived from the FOV and the distortion between the stated angles is modelled. Module editor with datasheet fields, projection models (rectilinear, equidistant, equisolid, stereographic, measured Kannala–Brandt), a live r(θ) plot, distortion table, image circle on the sensor, derived values, datasheet-vs-model FOV check and the source of every figure; its Preset list switches cameras to any library module and lens. Import from JSON, CSV or OpenCV YAML. |

| Housings block the cameras (Analysis ▸ Housings block cameras; Inspector ▸ Mount ▸ Housings) | Every lidar, radar, GNSS antenna and IMU housing (the library model's box or cylinder at its pose) and every other camera's module (width × height × length behind the lens) blocks each camera like the body: ground coverage, blind area, redundancy, density, obstacles, blind volume, perception range, the synthetic captures and sample-dataset images (housings drawn as dark plastic and metal), the stitched and bowl views, the camera light and the installation check all see them; a radar behind the bumper fascia or a module sunk into a panel does not show. | 3D rendering | Shader renderer (GLSL 1.20, falls back to fixed-function GL, then to QPainter): geometry kept on the GPU, smooth normals with crease angles, lighting in linear space with a gentle highlight roll-off (data colours such as heatmaps and camera light keep their exact legend colours), materials from the mesh's parts and zones (clear-coated paint with a metallic base lobe, reflecting a studio environment with three long overhead softboxes; dark see-through glass, matte tyres, black trim, light lenses, rig metal), sky occlusion that darkens wheel arches, mirrors and where sensors and rig parts meet the roof (rebuilt only when the geometry changes), soft Poisson-filtered shadows that the body, the rig and every sensor housing cast on the ground and on each other, a smooth contact shadow under the body, a studio backdrop on the real horizon (flat for top views, photos and the bowl), a floor grid drawn by the ground shader (anti-aliased 0.5 m and 1 m lines from the display frame's origin that thin out with distance, the frame's x axis red and y axis green; View ▸ Overlays ▸ Grid), ground maps with smooth anti-aliased edges, MSAA. **Camera light** tints the body's diffuse shading only (reflections, highlights and a thin rim in the camera's colour stay on top), with edges anti-aliased; on the ground it is exactly the legend colour inside the analysis ROI and fades over 6 m outside it to 15 % of its strength. With **Shadows and lighting** off the floor and backdrop stay; only the shadow and sky-occlusion maps go. **Labels** (View ▸ Overlays): camera tags and target names on small plates, a plate that would cover a nearer one left out. Sensors and cameras are drawn at their real size and pose: a lidar with its window band, a radar with its radome, a GNSS antenna as a dome, an IMU with its axes, each camera module with a lens ring in its colour; the selected one gets an outline. Overlays: radar zones as see-through volumes fading out to 15 m; **Lidar points** (one simulated sweep per lidar, computed in the background: returns coloured by range, beams the body, the rig or another housing stops in red — on the sample 36 % of the roof lidar's beams); **GNSS sky** (a dome over each antenna coloured where the GNSS study finds the sky blocked, with its open share); **Mount brackets** (generated brackets on the vehicle in their camera's colour). The QPainter fallback draws the same points, outlines and overlays in a simpler form. **Camera light** (View ▸ Overlays, Alt+3): every camera's real lens model and body occlusion evaluated per pixel, so the body and the ground show what each camera sees (overlaps hatched) live while a camera is dragged; the software renderer computes the same on the CPU. Animated view changes; **Render image…** (Ctrl+Shift+R, up to 4× the view size, vector overlays stay crisp) and **Turntable video…** (MP4 with ffmpeg, else PNG frames). |

| Studies | Monte Carlo tolerance study with sensitivity tornado and pinned worst cases; evolutionary placement optimiser scored under tolerance with a Pareto front; side-by-side variant comparison with Δ density map; calibration target planner with auto-place and suggestions; reference-photo matching (spin scrubber, landmarks, OpenCV calibration, matched 3D view). |
| Gradient refinement (Analysis ▸ Optimise placement… ▸ Refine with gradients) | After the evolutionary search, tunes every camera's x, y, z, yaw, pitch and roll (and optionally the stitch seams' angles) continuously: L-BFGS-B with torch gradients on a smooth surrogate of the coverage objective (soft in-view through the real lens model, soft line of sight from the engine's cube depth maps, soft density threshold; coverage 1 − Π(1 − p)), τ annealed from soft to sharp. The objective weighs blind ground, ground below the minimum density, unresolved critical targets and critical targets or obstacles that a stitch seam passes near. Cameras stay on their mount zone (penalised off the surface, re-projected after every stage), locked cameras stay, mirrored pairs move as one. Starts from the current variant or the selected Pareto candidates; every layout is checked with the exact engine and the best exact one kept. On the sample's data rig the blind ground falls from 0.183 to 0.145 m² (0.170 m² with the seam angles free, which also takes both poles off the seams); on the 4-cam variant the ground below 3 px/cm falls from 2.72 to 2.26 m² (moves of up to 50 mm and 8°). Iteration chart, before/after per camera, Add as variant (one undo step), JSON + CSV export; batch `--refine-rig` (`--refine-from`). Export format `diff_optim` (named only: not in the default export). |
| Installation check (Calibrate ▸ Installation check…, Ctrl+Shift+I) | One image per camera from a file, a live stream (V4L2 device, GStreamer pipeline, RTSP/HTTP URL; preview and grab) or a synthetic capture rendered from the plan with an optional simulated mounting error. The floor boards are cut out of each fisheye image as rectified pinhole patches (exact lens model), the chessboard corners found with OpenCV, and the installed pose solved by PnP on the bearing rays; boards that do not agree with the rest are dropped and the detection is repeated around the solved pose. Per camera: boards, corners, RMS, Δx Δy Δz, Δψ Δθ Δφ and a verdict against the tolerance (OK / adjust / re-mount). The captures are stitched into the bird's-eye view with the planned and with the measured poses side by side; save the report, the PNG, the remap tables, or apply the measured poses as an *as-built* variant. **Pick corners** is the fallback for a camera the detection fails on (glare, a board half hidden, a dirty lens): the camera's image is enlarged with a ring on every board corner where the plan (or the measured pose) puts it, and you drag the rings onto the real corners, with a 4× magnifier under the pointer (right-click takes a pick back). Four or more picked corners that span the floor give the whole pose, two or three the rotation with the position as planned. The picked pose then looks for the boards again: boards it finds replace their picks (their corners are far more precise), the others' picks stay in the solve. The result says how it was solved and less certain where it rests on picks, names a pick that lies far off the solve (the wrong corner, most likely), and a rotation-only camera stays out of the joint solve. Picks belong to their capture (a new image clears them), are saved in the report (`picked_corners`, each pick's error) and can be given to batch mode as `--install-picks`. |
| Live rig health (Calibrate ▸ Live rig…) | Every camera's stream at once, for the rig on the vehicle before a collection drive. Each camera's source is saved with the project and shared with the installation check's live field: an RTSP/HTTP URL, a V4L2 device, a GStreamer pipeline, a video file, or *Synthetic* (the parking scene rendered through the camera's lens at the module's frame rate, or one typed in, with sensor noise; a stand-in until the hardware is fitted). A password in a stream URL is not saved in the project: it goes to `stream-credentials.json` in the settings folder (readable only by you, keyed by user@host:port) and is put back when the stream is opened, so a shared project carries no camera passwords; labels, the history and reports show it as `***`. Per camera, from frame arrival times and the images: measured frame rate against the module's, interval jitter, dropped frames (gaps over 1.5 frame intervals), age of the newest frame, resolution against the module's sensor, mean brightness, clipped share, sharpness (variance of the Laplacian, for a blurred, fogged or covered lens) and frozen images, with an OK / warning / fault verdict and its reasons; for the rig the cameras up, the worst frame rate, faults and the sync skew between cameras, judged against the sync plan's trigger offsets (a rig synchronised to a spinning lidar fires its cameras round the sweep: that stagger is no skew; streams from the CARLA live rig are judged against its own schedule) and taken from software timestamps (when frames reached Vantage, not sensor time). Over each image the planned body outline and horizon through the lens model, and an edge alignment indicator (median distance of the planned body edge to the edge in the image) that flags a camera that may have moved; an indicator, not a measurement. Readers run on their own threads and reconnect with back-off (1, 2, 4 … 15 s); the page stops them when hidden. **Save frames** writes `<tag>.png` per camera and `rig_health.json`; **Check installation** hands the frames to the installation check. All thresholds are in `HealthSettings` (`vantage/core/rig_health.py`). |
| Calibration coach (Calibrate ▸ Live rig… ▸ Calibration coach) | Guides a technician holding a checkerboard in front of one live camera until the calibration is pinned. Lens: each sharp, new view (the lens calibration's own detector; sharpness from rig health; a view within 10 cm and 8° of one taken is a repeat) adds its corners' Fisher information on the Kannala–Brandt intrinsics, each view's board pose marginalised; six bars (focal x, y, centre x, y, distortion, all rays) show the 1σ ray error they leave over the part of the image a hand-held board can reach and turn green within the requirement (the scorecard's, else the calibration observability's 0.1°). Lidar–camera: the same for where the lidar sits relative to the camera, with the calibration observability's model of the lidar–camera calibration (equal to its figures to 1e-6). The next view is the hand-held pose (0.5–2 m high, tilted up to 50°, the whole board in the image with squares of 8 px or more) that firms up the loosest bar most; it is drawn as a ghost board through the exact lens model over the live image and said in words ("move 0.6 m to your left, tilt the top of the board 20° towards the camera") and in vehicle coordinates. Finish hands the views to Lens calibration, or writes them (with the lidar's scans when simulated) as image + point-cloud pairs for Lidar–camera calibration. A simulated technician (coached or random, seeded) shows rendered boards to a synthetic stream. On the sample's data rig, following the coach pins the front fisheye's lens to 0.1° in 25 views where five random hand-held sessions do not get there in 40 (2.4× at 40), and the rear camera–roof lidar pair to 0.3° and 10 mm in 8 views against a median of 21. Export: JSON (`vantage.calib_coach/1`) + views CSV (named only: not in the default export); batch `--calib-coach`. |
| Unit lens calibration (Calibrate ▸ Lens calibration…, Ctrl+Shift+L) | Every camera can carry its own intrinsics instead of the library lens: calibrate from 10–20 views of a hand-held checkerboard (image files, a live stream, or a synthetic set rendered through a simulated unit), fitted as cv::fisheye (Kannala–Brandt, fx/fy/cx/cy/k1–k4). Boards the raw fisheye distorts too much are found in rectified views and mapped back exactly; poor views are dropped. Shows per-view RMS, corner coverage over the image with advice, parameters against the library lens, and how far the library lens would put a ray on this unit. Import calibrations made elsewhere (OpenCV YAML/XML, ROS camera_info, Kalibr camchain, JSON), matched to cameras by name, topic, file name or serial. The unit calibration replaces the library lens in the analysis, the installation check, the remap tables and every export (which says so). |
| Joint extrinsic solve | The installation check refines all cameras together with each board's placement on the floor (x, y, yaw under a placement prior) in one sparse least-squares problem, so cameras that share a board agree at the seams even when the boards were laid a centimetre or two off. It reports the seam agreement before and after (floor disagreement of corners seen by two cameras), the estimated board positions, names boards that lie beyond their tolerance, and sets aside a board observation no placement explains. |
| Lidar–camera calibration (Calibrate ▸ Lidar–camera calibration…) | Where a lidar sits relative to a camera, and so in the vehicle, measured from a printed checkerboard held up at several poses both see, against the plan. The pair is ranked by how much of the camera's image the lidar's beams cover. The **pose plan** places each board where both sensors see all of it, at least four lidar channels cross it and its squares stay big enough in the image, turned (yaw, pitch, roll) for what its plane adds to pinning the lidar's six degrees of freedom, and shows the uncertainty the plan can reach. Captures are **synthetic** (the camera image through its real lens, the lidar scan with the model's beams and range noise, with an optional injected mounting error to see the recovery) or a **folder** of real image + point-cloud pairs (PCD, KITTI .bin, nuScenes .pcd.bin, PLY, .npy, CSV). The camera finds the corners through the exact lens model; the lidar gives the board plane, its edge (bracketed between the last beam on the board and the first beam past it) and, when its reflectivity shows them, the squares. One robust solve of the extrinsic and every board's pose gives T_camera_lidar with its 1σ, Δ against the plan in the camera and vehicle frames, a verdict against the tolerances, a moved board set aside, and a set of boards that cannot pin a direction named as such ("the boards were all tilted the same way: the translation along z is not determined"). The lidar's points drawn on the image through the plan and the solved extrinsic are the visual proof. Never a path, user or host name of the computer. `vantage project.vantage --reproduce result.json` runs the same study on the same project again and compares every number, exactly or within `--tolerance` per quantity (`blind_m2=0.005`, `'*_pct=0.1%'`), and says why a result does not reproduce: another version, other code or libraries, which part of the project differs, a changed input file, or an edited result. Every random number comes from a seeded generator (a test reads the sources), and studies give the same numbers bit for bit in two processes, whatever the random ids of the cameras and boards. The assistant's `check_result` reads a result's block without running anything. The `.vantage` format is documented in `docs/project-format.md` with a JSON Schema; `CITATION.cff` says how to cite Vantage. |

Conventions: ISO 8855 (x forward, y left, z up), origin at the rear-axle centre on the ground; camera orientation is intrinsic
Z-Y-X yaw/pitch/roll with pitch positive down. Pixel density is √(radial × tangential sampling) / range, in px/cm.
Exported and imported intrinsics use OpenCV pixel coordinates (pixel centres on integers: the centre of a 1920-wide
sensor is cx = 959.5); the remap tables use sensor coordinates with pixel centres at i + 0.5, as their header says.
