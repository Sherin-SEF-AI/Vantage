# Vantage · sensor rig planner for autonomous-driving data collection

A desktop app for designing the sensor rigs of data-collection vehicles, the cars that record the camera data
self-driving models are trained on. Import a 3D model of the vehicle, place the cameras on it, and prove in simulation
what the rig will record before any hardware is fitted: what each camera sees through its real lens model, where the
body, the rig and the other sensors' housings block it, how many pixels land on the ground and on objects, where cameras overlap, and how sensitive the layout is
to mounting errors and load. Then calibrate the real cameras from their live streams (RTSP/HTTP, V4L2, GStreamer
pipelines such as a Jetson's GMSL cameras), check every installed camera against the plan, check a recorded drive of the
real rig against the plan (does each camera see the vehicle where the simulation says it does, and do the coverage
figures still hold as built?), and hand the calibration to the data pipeline (OpenCV, ROS 2, Kalibr, nuScenes, KITTI and
more, each checked against the tool that reads it). Lidars and radars sit on the rig next to the
cameras: where their beams and fields reach, what the vehicle blocks, how far they detect each object, how their
timing lines up with the cameras, and how much data the whole rig records.

Then drive the planned rig in the CARLA simulator: every camera frame and lidar sweep comes labelled (semantic,
instance and range images through each fisheye's own lens model, 2D and 3D boxes, COCO, nuScenes lidarseg and panoptic),
with faults of known size, standard scenarios, detection quality per rig and campaigns that measure how small a fault
Vantage's own checks catch. Or just say what you want run: the **Copilot** (Groq or Claude, with your own key) turns it
into a plan with every value and its reason, and nothing runs until you approve it.

The same engine also serves production surround-view systems: stitched bird's-eye remap tables, a 3D bowl view, parking
guidelines and where standing people vanish at stitch seams.

**Website and documentation:** <https://sherin-sef-ai.github.io/Vantage/> (feature videos recorded from the app, the full documentation, batch-mode reference). The site's source is `site/`; it is published by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`.

It opens with the **Tata Tigor EV** sample: the body is lofted from the profile measured on the 19-frame studio spin
(wheel-centre calibration, 1.6 px RMS), the cameras are e-con Systems STURDeCAM31 (Sony ISX031, 1920 × 1536, 3.0 µm) with
the H05R2 wide and H03R2 narrow lenses, and five layout variants are included; the fifth, *Data rig*, adds a
128-channel roof lidar and five radars to the six cameras.

The studio photos themselves are not part of the repository. To use the photo-matching features with them, put
`Tata-Tigor-EV-360.zip` in `data/` (or next to the project folder) or set `VANTAGE_TIGOR_PHOTOS` to its path; without
it the sample opens normally with the photo poses but no images.

## Install

Download the file for your system from the [latest release](https://github.com/Sherin-SEF-AI/Vantage/releases/latest).
Each one carries its own Python and libraries, so there is nothing else to install.

| System | File | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | `Vantage-<version>-windows-x64-setup.exe` (installer) or `…-windows-x64.zip` | Windows 10 or 11, x64 |
| macOS | `Vantage-<version>-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon |
| Linux | `Vantage-<version>-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` | x86_64, glibc 2.35 or later (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 and newer) |
| Linux arm64 | `Vantage-<version>-linux-arm64.tar.gz` | arm64, glibc 2.35 or later: NVIDIA Jetson Orin with JetPack 6, Ubuntu 22.04 arm64 and newer |

**Windows.** Run the setup. It installs for the current user without administrator rights (into
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Vantage`), adds Vantage and *Vantage self-test* to the Start menu, and opens `.vantage`
projects. The installer is not signed, so SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC": choose **More info**, then
**Run anyway**. `Vantage.exe` is the window; `vantage-cli.exe` in the same folder is for batch mode and `--self-test`
from a terminal. The `.zip` is the same program without the installer: unzip it anywhere and run `Vantage.exe`.
Uninstall from *Settings ▸ Apps*.

**macOS.** Unzip the file and move `Vantage.app` to Applications. The app is not signed or notarised, so macOS refuses
the first start: open *System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security* and choose **Open Anyway** next to the message about
Vantage. On macOS 14 you can instead Control-click the app and choose **Open**. If macOS says the app is damaged, run
`xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Vantage.app` in Terminal. Batch mode and the self-test:
`/Applications/Vantage.app/Contents/MacOS/vantage-cli --self-test`.

**Linux.**

```bash
tar xf Vantage-*-linux-x86_64.tar.gz      # or …-linux-arm64.tar.gz on an arm64 machine such as a Jetson
cd Vantage-*-linux-*/
./install.sh            # per user: ~/.local/opt/vantage, a menu entry, the .vantage file type, ~/.local/bin/vantage
vantage --self-test     # checks the analysis, the exports, cameras, ROS 2 bags, Qt's plugins, OpenGL and every page
```

`~/.local/opt/vantage/uninstall.sh` removes it again (projects and settings are kept). Without installing, run
`./Vantage` from the extracted folder.

**On the vehicle (NVIDIA Jetson).** The arm64 bundle runs on a Jetson Orin with JetPack 6, so the live rig health, the
drive-log check and the as-built check run on the data-collection computer itself. Cameras open as V4L2 devices
(`/dev/video0`, as e-con's GMSL drivers expose them) or as GStreamer pipelines such as
`nvarguscamerasrc sensor-id=0 ! nvvidconv ! video/x-raw,format=BGRx ! appsink`: the bundle's OpenCV has no GStreamer,
so Vantage runs the pipeline in the system's `gst-launch-1.0` (JetPack's, with NVIDIA's plug-ins) and reads the frames
back. `vantage --self-test` says which of these work on the machine. JetPack 5 (Ubuntu 20.04) is too old for the
bundle: run from source there.

**Checking a download.** `SHA256SUMS.txt` in the release lists the SHA-256 of every file:

```bash
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS.txt --ignore-missing                  # Linux: checks the files you downloaded
shasum -a 256 Vantage-*-macos-arm64.zip                         # macOS: compare with its line in SHA256SUMS.txt
```

On Windows, `Get-FileHash .\Vantage-<version>-windows-x64-setup.exe` in PowerShell prints the hash to compare.

`vantage --self-test` (on Windows the *Vantage self-test* shortcut) checks an installation without opening a window:
it opens the sample, runs an analysis, writes every export format and a ROS 2 bag to a temporary folder, loads Qt's
platform plugins, draws with OpenGL and with the software renderer, and builds every page. It ends with
`self-test passed`, or names what is missing.

**With Python 3.10 or later:**

```bash
python3 -m pip install .            # from this folder; add [step] for STEP import through gmsh,
                                    # [bags] for compressed ROS 2 bags (zstd, lz4), [keyring] for the assistant's key,
                                    # [claude] for the Claude provider (Anthropic's SDK: or pip install --user anthropic)
vantage                             # the window (vantage-gui on Windows starts it without a console)
vantage --install-desktop           # Linux: Vantage in the app menu and dock with its icon, and .vantage files
vantage --desktop-shortcut          # Linux: the same, plus a Vantage launcher on the desktop
vantage project.vantage --analyse report.json
```

**Building the downloads.** Tag a version (`git tag v0.6.0 && git push origin v0.6.0`). The *Build installers*
workflow builds and smoke-tests all four bundles (Linux x86_64 and arm64, Windows, macOS). When all pass, it publishes
the GitHub release with `SHA256SUMS.txt` and the CHANGELOG entry as notes. Starting the workflow by hand from the Actions tab builds the
bundles as artifacts only, without a release. To build for the machine you are on, in a fresh virtual environment
(the bundle requirements take OpenCV without its GUI):

```bash
python3 -m venv build-env && . build-env/bin/activate
python -m pip install -r packaging/requirements-bundle.txt
python packaging/build.py --smoke   # dist/: the bundle, the archive and on Windows the installer (needs Inno Setup 6)
```

## Run

```bash
cd vantage
python3 -m pip install -r requirements.txt     # PyQt6, numpy, scipy, PyOpenGL, PyYAML, opencv-python
python3 run_vantage.py                          # start window
python3 run_vantage.py --sample                 # straight into the Tigor EV sample
python3 run_vantage.py car.glb                  # import a model
python3 run_vantage.py project.vantage          # open a project
```

Options: `--theme dark|light|system`, `--scale 1.15`, `--software-3d` (QPainter 3D view instead of OpenGL),
`--template sedan|hatchback|suv|van|truck|tigor`, `--self-test`, `--version`.

`--variant NAME|N` picks a variant, `--frame`, `--units m|mm` and `--angles deg|rad` set the export conventions, and
`--pos-tol`, `--ang-tol`, `--min-boards` the installation tolerances. The installation check solves all cameras jointly
with the board placement unless `--no-joint`; `--board-tol MM` is how far a board may lie off its planned place
(default 20). The stitched PNG is exposure-balanced unless `--no-balance`, `--blend multiband` for multi-band seams.
`--rig-health SECONDS [OUT.json]` reads the streams set on the Live rig page (saved in the project), waits for their
first frames, then measures for SECONDS and writes `vantage.rig_health/1` (default stdout). `--condition ID`
(clear, overcast, night, night_lit, light_rain, heavy_rain, fog or snow) sets the weather `--perception-range`,
`--dataset` and `--weather` use (default: the project's). `--watch PROJECT.vantage` regenerates the formats of the project's automation settings
(Export ▸ Automation; `--export DIR`, `--formats` and `--variant` override them) once at the start and after every
change of the file, runs `--on-change` through the shell in the project's folder, and stops after `--watch-runs N`
runs (default: when stopped). The command comes from the command line only, never from the project file.
`--reproduce RESULT.json [OUT.json]` runs again what wrote a result (from its provenance block: the same project given
first, input files next to the result or in this folder) and compares every number, exactly or within
`--tolerance [PATTERN=]VALUE[%]` per quantity. `--experiment SPEC.json OUT_DIR` runs a designed experiment exported
from Analysis ▸ Experiments and resumes from OUT_DIR's journal after an interruption. `--validate [OUT_DIR]` writes
the validation suite's report (default `./validation`; with a project its vehicle and exports are checked too),
`--validate-models lens,weather` picks models, `--validate-quick` is a 30-second smoke run. `--carla-rig OUT_DIR`
writes the variant as the CARLA bridge reads it; `--carla-run OUT_DIR` drives it in the CARLA server already running on
the port of Simulate ▸ CARLA's settings (`--carla-python PY`: CARLA's client Python, else the one the page chose).
With either, `--carla-scenario NAME` and `--carla-scenario-params JSON`, `--carla-faults FAULTS.json`,
`--carla-actors ACTORS.json` (a file or the JSON itself; `none` clears the project's), `--carla-ground-truth
all|none|semantic,instance,range,lidar_labels`, `--carla-walkers N` and `--carla-spawn-point N` go into the rig over
the project's CARLA settings, each checked against the rig first. `--ground-truth-summary RUN_DIR [OUT.json]` reads a
drive's ground truth (no project needed). `--carla-live SECONDS [OUT_DIR]` drives the cameras live (`--live-cameras
F,R`, `--live-scale 0.25`, `--live-fault AT_S:JSON` repeatable) and writes rig health's verdicts (`rig_health.json`) and
the live server's report. `--detection-quality OUT_DIR` evaluates the detector on the project's recordings or on
`--detection-recording RUN_DIR` (repeatable, the first the reference); `--detection-download` fetches the model first.
`--campaign OUT_DIR` runs the project's simulation campaign or `--campaign-template
fault_detectability|weather_sweep|rig_ab`, its drives in the campaign's own folder when it names one, else
`OUT_DIR/drives`, and resumes from what is done; `--campaign-phase drive|evaluate|analyse` runs part of it (only
`drive` needs CARLA). `--critical-carla OUT_DIR` stands the critical targets in CARLA (drives in `OUT_DIR/drives`),
`--critical-carla-drives DIR` compares drives recorded before without CARLA (one drive, or the drives at any depth
under DIR, each named by its path there). `--run-plan ID[:VERSION] [OUT_DIR]` runs a Copilot plan approved in the
app from the approval record in the project (its stored settings; no model is asked), each step through the option
that runs that kind into `OUT_DIR/step-<n>-<kind>` (default: the plan's folder in the working folder), every output's
provenance naming the plan; CARLA steps need a server already running, as `--carla-run` does. A modifier without its
option is refused.
`--export` without `--formats` leaves out the experiment, the CARLA rig, the simulation campaign and the detection
quality (minutes, tens of MB); the last two export what is on disk (the campaign's drives and checks, the recordings'
cached detections) and drive nothing.
Captures are matched to cameras by tag in the file name (`F.png`, `cam_QR.jpg`, `ML-capture.tif`); imported
calibrations by the camera name or ROS topic in the file, the file name, or the serial number. Exit code: 0 fine, 2 the
plan has critical issues (with `--analyse` or `--export`; with `--recording-budget`, the recorder or a sensor's link
cannot keep up; with `--power-budget`, the DC-DC converter or a fuse is over its rating; with `--gnss`, an antenna has no
sky; with `--brackets`, a camera cannot take a printed bracket as planned; with `--suspension`, a load case lifts a wheel
or exceeds the gross vehicle mass; with `--housings`, a camera sits inside a housing; with `--critical-targets`, no camera
resolves a critical target; with `--materials`, a radar looks into metal over half its field; with `--terrain`, the body
or a sensor would touch the road or no camera resolves a speed bump or kerb; with `--harness`, a cable is longer than its
link allows, no module cable reaches or a GNSS cable has no RF margin; with `--observability` and calibration targets
planned, a camera sees no board whole), 3 a camera has to be re-mounted or could not
be checked (or, with `--rig-health`, a camera's stream is faulty or not set; with `--drive-log`, the recording shows a
fault; with `--as-built-check`, the plan's coverage does not hold as built; with `--fleet`, a sensor needs re-mounting in its vehicle's latest record; with `--lidar-calib`, the lidar has to be
re-mounted or its pose is not determined), 4 a lens calibration failed or an
imported calibration matched no camera (or did not fit it), or a fleet record, drive log, as-built recording, suspension data file or lidar calibration capture could not be read or the
sample dataset not written (or the `--experiment` spec cannot be read or does not fit the project, or `--reproduce`
cannot try the result), 5 with `--check` a must requirement fails or cannot be evaluated, 6 with `--reproduce` the
result does not reproduce or with `--validate` a check of the models fails, 7 with `--carla-run`, `--carla-live`,
`--campaign` or `--critical-carla` no CARLA server answers, CARLA's client Python is missing or the run fails (2 with
`--carla-rig` or `--carla-run` when the variant has nothing to simulate or a `--carla-*` option does not fit it, or
its cameras would render more than the bridge's 200 Mpx a frame; with
`--ground-truth-summary` when the drive has no ground truth; with `--detection-quality` when there is no recording with
ground truth to evaluate; with `--campaign` when the design has nothing to drive or names a sensor the variant lacks;
with `--critical-carla` when no target can be stood in CARLA or no drive is found; 3 with `--carla-live` when rig health
finds a camera faulty and with `--critical-carla` when a camera view disagrees with the study), 8 with
`--detection-quality` (or a `--campaign` whose checks include detection) the detector model is missing or does not
verify, 9 with `--campaign` a drive failed or is still to do (run it again: what is done is reused), 10 with
`--run-plan` when the project holds no approved, intact plan of that ID or a step no longer validates (approve it again
in the app; otherwise `--run-plan` returns the first failing step's own code, and a step that reports findings, 3, 5 or
6, lets the plan go on); when several apply, the highest is returned. Ctrl+C during a CARLA run returns 130 once CARLA is tidied up. `--watch` exits with its last run's code: the command's own when it ran, else 5 when the
requirements check fails, 4 when the project could not be read or a format not written, 130 when stopped during the
command.

## What is in it

| Area | Features |
|---|---|
| Vehicle import (Ctrl+I) | .glb .gltf .obj .stl .ply .fbx (binary and ASCII), .step via the optional gmsh package. Wizard: units (guessed from the bounding box), up/forward axes and mirroring, inside-out meshes flipped automatically, wheel centres (auto from wheel parts or picked on the mesh), ground plane, origin preset, spec check against datasheet dimensions, occluder simplification, mount zones painted with a brush, glass detected from materials and transparency. |
| Rig (Vehicle ▸ Add rig part) | The data-collection rig on the vehicle as simple solids: **roof rack** (two crossbars and a platform), **sensor mast** (an upright tube with a head 0.5 m above the roof), **sensor enclosure** and **crossbar**, each sized and placed from the vehicle's roof and added as one undo step. Parts are boxes, upright cylinders or bars across the vehicle (centre, size, yaw). A part that *occludes* blocks cameras exactly like the body (ground coverage, blind volume, camera images, the installation check's synthetic captures, the assistant's *why is this point not seen*, which names the part); a *mountable* one carries cameras (place on it, drag along it with surface snapping; a camera on a part that is not mountable is flagged as on a forbidden zone). Listed in the Outliner (select, hide, delete), edited in the Inspector, drawn in 3D and as outlines in the top view, picked with a click; saved with the project and exported as fixed links with box/cylinder geometry in the ROS 2 URDF, a `rig` list in the extrinsics and a table in the report. |
| Lidars, radars, GNSS and IMU (Vehicle ▸ Add lidar / Add radar / Add GNSS antenna / Add IMU) | Lidars and radars on the rig next to the cameras, per variant. The library (nominal figures from public datasheets, each with its source note; check them against your unit's): **Ouster OS1-128** (Rev 7, 128 channels, ±22.5°, 90 m on a 10 % target), **Hesai OT128** (128 ch, −25° to +15°, 200 m), **Hesai AT128** (fixed 120° × 25.4° field, 200 m), **Hesai XT32** (32 ch, 1° spacing, 80 m), **Velodyne VLP-16** (16 ch, ±15°, 100 m), **Livox Mid-360** (non-repetitive 360° × 59°, 40 m, modelled as an even grid with the same points per frame), and three typical 77 GHz radar classes (long-range with a far and a near beam, 4D imaging, corner), which are classes, not products. A lidar goes on top of the highest mountable rig part (a mast head) or the roof, a radar in the middle of the front bumper; then move it in the Inspector (optical centre, yaw/pitch/roll like a camera), drag it in the top view, or pick another model. A lidar is a channel fan (beam elevations, azimuth step, frame rate, range on a 10 % target, points per second, bytes per point, spin direction); a radar is one or more zones (range on a car, ±azimuth, ±elevation), smaller targets reaching the fourth root of their radar cross-section. Editing a built-in model's figures makes an edited copy for that sensor; **Import beam angles…** reads the measured channel elevations from an Ouster metadata JSON, a Hesai angle-correction CSV, a Velodyne ROS calibration YAML or a plain list. Drawn in 3D (housing; the lidar's ground rings, where its downward beams meet the ground; the radar's field edges) and in the top view (rings, radar fields, footprints, tags), picked with a click, listed in the Outliner (select, hide, delete). The Issues list flags a lidar inside the body or a rig part, a radar deeper than 15 cm behind the bumper surface, a sensor below the ground and a tag a camera also uses. Saved with the variant; exported as frames (x forward, y left, z up at the optical centre) in the extrinsics (with the field and spin), the ROS 2 URDF (links with the housing) and static TF launch file, the nuScenes `sensor` / `calibrated_sensor` tables (`LIDAR_<TAG>`, `RADAR_<TAG>`), KITTI (`Tr_<tag>_to_vehicle`, and `calib_velo_to_cam.txt` for the first lidar into camera 00) and the report. **GNSS antennas and IMUs** sit on the rig the same way: an antenna's position is its phase centre (a typical survey antenna and a compact patch antenna in the library), an IMU's the centre of its axes and its frame its own axes (typical dual-antenna MEMS GNSS/INS, FOG GNSS/INS and MEMS IMU classes, with heading error at a stated baseline, RTK position error, roll and pitch error, output rate, antenna inputs, and the accelerometer and gyro noise densities and bias random walks that Kalibr's `imu.yaml` takes; not products: enter your unit's figures). The first antenna goes near the rear edge of the roof (or the rack over it), the second near the front edge for the longest heading baseline, the IMU on the cabin floor above the rear axle; the Inspector shows each antenna's baseline and the INS's heading error for it, the 3D and top views draw the baseline and the IMU's axes. The Issues list flags an antenna inside the body or a rig part (no sky); an IMU belongs inside. They are exported in the extrinsics, the URDF and TF, KITTI (`Tr_<tag>_to_vehicle`, and `calib_imu_to_velo.txt` from the first IMU into the first lidar) and the report; nuScenes has no GNSS or IMU channels (they make its `ego_pose`).
