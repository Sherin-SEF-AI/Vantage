Sensor rig planner · autonomous-driving data collection

Design data-collection rigs that record what your models need.

Vantage designs the sensor rigs of the vehicles that record training data for self-driving models. Place the cameras, lidars and radars on the vehicle and prove in simulation what the rig will record, before any hardware is fitted: what every lens sees, where the body blocks it, how many pixels land on the road, what each lidar and radar reaches, and whether the recorder keeps up. Then calibrate the real cameras from their live streams and hand the calibration to your data pipeline.

From CAD to calibrated rig

One tool from the first sketch to the first drive.

A data-collection rig is only as good as its weakest camera. Vantage covers the work from placing the cameras to checking them on the vehicle, so the numbers you plan with are the numbers you verify against.

Import the vehicle

glTF, OBJ, STL, PLY, FBX or STEP. A wizard sorts out units, axes, wheels, glass and the zones where cameras may go.

Place the sensors

Snap camera modules from the library onto the body, mirror them in pairs and aim them; add lidars and radars; watch what the rig sees update as you go.

Simulate the recording

Simulated camera images, coverage, pixel density, overlaps, the 3D blind volume, lidar and radar reach, trigger timing and the data budget, before anything is bought or drilled.

Fit, calibrate, verify

Calibrate each camera from its live stream, check every installed camera against the plan, watch the streams' health, and export the calibration in your dataset's format.

Features

Know what the rig will record, before anything is drilled.

All videos on this page were recorded from the application itself, running the bundled Tata Tigor EV sample with six fisheye cameras.

Simulation

Coverage, resolution and redundancy, live

The ground around the vehicle is evaluated cell by cell for every camera: inside the lens's field of view, inside the image frame, not hidden by the body. For a data-collection rig that answers where the recording has holes, where it is too coarse to label, and where two cameras overlap. The result updates while you work.

  • Blind ground, coverage and redundancy on a 5, 2 or 1 cm grid inside an ROI drawn around the body or as a polygon.
  • Pixel density of the best camera at every point, in px/cm, against your minimum and target.
  • Per camera: what it sees, what only it sees, and what goes blind if it fails or is mounted badly.
  • An Issues list that checks cable lengths, GMSL ports and link budget, mounting zones and edge clearance.
Placement

Place cameras on the body, not in a spreadsheet

Cameras snap to the vehicle's surface with their housing standoff, and left/right pairs stay mirrored. Every field takes expressions and units, so 1880/2 mm means what it says.

  • Place on the surface (C), mirrored pairs with Alt-click, move (G) and rotate (R) gizmos, aim at a ground point.
  • A library of camera modules and lenses: rectilinear, fisheye models and measured Kannala–Brandt, with a datasheet field-of-view check.
  • An editable camera table with paste from Excel, coordinate frames from the rear axle, front axle or bumper, in m or mm.
  • Undo for everything, including scrubbing a value with the mouse as one step.
3D view

Camera light: each lens's view, painted on the car

Turn on Camera light (Alt+3) and every camera lights up what it can see on the body and the ground, through its own lens model and around the body that occludes it. Overlaps show where two cameras meet.

  • A GPU shader renderer with shadows, contact shadow and smooth shading; it falls back to plain OpenGL and then to a software renderer.
  • Frustums, footprints, occlusion rays, seams and dimensions as overlays.
  • Render image at up to four times the view size and turntable videos for reviews.
3D analysis

The blind volume: where something can stand unseen

Ground coverage is not the whole story. Vantage samples the space around the car level by level and shows, in 3D, every pocket no camera sees, and how close to the body a pole, a child or an adult can stand without being detected.

  • Blind volume, the height up to which the ground is covered, and the undetected area for each object height.
  • A polar chart of how far from the body something can hide in every direction.
  • A height slider over a plan slice, and the blind space cut away in 3D at that height.
Perception range

How far out the rig resolves cars, people and signs

A detector needs an object to be a number of pixels tall. Vantage stands each object class at every distance in every direction and checks it through each camera's real lens and around the body: inside the image, not hidden, and tall enough. The result is the range at which your dataset will contain usable examples.

  • Car, pedestrian, cyclist and traffic sign with real sizes and your detector's minimum pixel height; add your own classes.
  • The range per direction against the range you need per sector, with the worst direction in each sector flagged. On the sample's six fisheyes a car is resolved to 70 m ahead, short of the default 80 m.
  • Camera pairs that overlap, with their baseline and the depth resolution a stereo pair would give at 10, 20 and 50 m.
  • Exported as JSON and a PNG plan; in batch mode with --perception-range.
Rig parts

Roof racks and masts that block and carry cameras

A data-collection vehicle carries more than its body: crossbars, a roof rack, a sensor mast, an enclosure. Add them from the Vehicle menu, sized to the roof, and they hide what they hide in every analysis: the ground coverage, the blind volume, the camera images and the synthetic captures.

  • Boxes, cylinders and bars with position, size and yaw. Each part blocks the view, carries cameras, or both.
  • Cameras snap onto the parts that may carry them; a part marked as not mountable is a forbidden zone in the Issues list.
  • The rig goes out with the design: links in the ROS 2 URDF, a list in the extrinsics and a table in the report.
Lidar, radar and the recorder

The whole rig, not just its cameras.

A collection vehicle carries lidars and radars next to its cameras, and a recorder that has to keep up with all of them. Vantage places them on the same model, shows what they reach and what the vehicle blocks, times the cameras to the lidar's sweep and adds up the data before anything is bought.

Lidars and radars

Lidars and radars on the rig

Add a lidar or a radar from the Vehicle menu. A lidar goes on the mast head or the roof, a radar in the front bumper; move them in the inspector or drag them in the top view. The 3D view draws where each lidar channel meets the ground and each radar's field.

  • Ouster OS1-128, Hesai OT128, AT128 and XT32, Velodyne VLP-16, Livox Mid-360, and typical long-range, imaging and corner radars. Figures are nominal datasheet values, each with its source; edit them to match your unit.
  • Import the measured beam angles from an Ouster metadata file, a Hesai angle-correction CSV or a Velodyne calibration.
  • The Issues list flags a lidar inside the body and a radar set deeper than a bumper fascia.
  • Every sensor frame goes into the URDF and TF launch file, the extrinsics, the nuScenes tables and KITTI, including the lidar-to-camera file KITTI tools expect.
Lidar and radar coverage

How many kinds of sensor see each object

For every direction and object class, how far the cameras, the lidars and the radars detect it, against the range your dataset needs. A sector two or more of them cover is redundant, one is single, none is a gap.

  • Lidar points counted on each object from the real beam elevations, around the body and the rig; radar ranges scaled by each object's cross-section.
  • What the vehicle blocks: on the sample, 35 % of a lidar sitting on the roof hits the roof, and its nearest ground point is 8.5 m or more from the body. Raised 0.6 m, it loses none.
  • How much of each camera image the lidar covers, for projecting points into images.
  • Exported as JSON and a PNG plan; in batch mode with --sensor-coverage.
Sync and timing

Cameras that fire when the lidar looks

Each camera should expose its image while the spinning lidar sweeps across its view. Vantage works out when the sweep crosses every camera, the trigger that centres a rolling-shutter exposure on it, and what sharing a trigger line costs.

  • A dial of one lidar rotation with every camera's field, sweep crossing and trigger.
  • Suggested trigger lines: on the sample's data rig, six cameras on one line are up to 12.4 ms off the sweep (17 cm at 50 km/h); four lines bring that under 1 ms.
  • Phases for further lidars, motion blur and rolling-shutter skew at speed, and the longest exposure that keeps blur under a pixel.
  • Exported as JSON and the dial; in batch mode with --sync-plan.

These are planned times. The Live rig page measures the real skew, from software timestamps.

Recording budget

Will the recorder keep up?

Every camera's, lidar's and radar's data rate, the load on each link and on the uplink to the recorder, the write speed the disks need, and how many hours of driving fit on a disk set.

  • Cameras raw, JPEG or H.265; lidars as driver packets or decoded clouds; GNSS, IMU and CAN on top.
  • On the sample, six raw cameras record 8.5 Gbit/s: an 8 TB disk set lasts 2 hours, which the Issues list reports.
  • A recorder short of write speed, or a lidar on a link it saturates, is a critical issue.
  • Exported as JSON and CSV; in batch mode with --recording-budget, which exits with 2 when something cannot keep up.

The JPEG and H.265 bits per pixel are assumptions: replace them with figures measured on your encoder.

Also for production surround-view systems

The same engine, for the in-car display.

If the rig, or the vehicle it prototypes, has a surround-view display, Vantage designs that too: the stitched bird's-eye view and its seams, the 3D bowl, parking guidelines and what stitching does to standing people.

Surround view

The in-car display, before the car exists

Vantage renders a synthetic parking scene through every camera's real lens, stitches the images into the bird's-eye view and projects them onto a 3D bowl around the car, exactly as a surround-view ECU would.

  • Seams you can drag, exposure balancing and multi-band blending; the bowl's floor, wall, reach and profile are tunable.
  • Remap tables per camera for the bird's-eye view, with a dependency-free C++17 and a Python reference stitcher that match Vantage to within a grey level.
  • Export the bowl as OBJ and texture with per-vertex remap tables for a 3D renderer on the ECU.
Parking guidelines

Trajectory lines and the swept path, through every lens

A kinematic model of the vehicle turns the steering angle into the lines a production display draws: distance markers, the bumper corners' trajectory in red, yellow and green, and the ground the whole body, mirrors included, passes over.

  • Drawn through each camera's real lens: they curve with the fisheye and disappear behind the body.
  • On the camera images, the bird's-eye view, the bowl and the 3D view; obstacles in the swept path are flagged with the distance to contact.
  • Exported per camera for the ECU: pixel polylines for every steering angle from lock to lock.
Stitched view

Where people vanish at the seams

A bird's-eye view assumes everything lies on the ground, so a standing person is smeared away from each camera. Near a seam both smears can land in the other camera's sector, and the person disappears from the display even though both raw images show them.

  • A pole, a 0.9 m child or a 1.8 m adult is stood on every point and followed through the same seams and blending as the remap tables.
  • The map separates vanishes at a seam from cut off by the view's edge, marks hotspots and shows the smears under the cursor.
  • Suggest seams searches each seam ±15° for angles that hide less, without losing ground density; Apply is one undo step.
Robustness

Designs that survive the real world.

A camera is never mounted exactly where it was drawn, and a loaded car sits lower. These studies tell you how much that matters, and which camera to worry about.

Tolerance, optimiser, load cases, variants

  • Monte Carlo tolerance study with a sensitivity tornado and the worst cases pinned for inspection.
  • Placement optimiser: an evolutionary search scored under tolerance, with a Pareto front.
  • Load cases: axle drops become body pitch and drop; for each case the blind area, density, the ground shift of the stitched image and the ghosting at the seams.
  • Variants side by side with a difference map and a table of every figure.

Fit and calibrate

Calibrate the real cameras from their live streams

Point Vantage at each camera's stream (an RTSP or HTTP URL, a V4L2 device, or a GStreamer pipeline such as a Jetson's GMSL cameras), lay the planned checkerboards on the floor, and check every installed camera against the plan from one frame each.

  • Boards are found in rectified patches cut out of the fisheye image with the exact lens model, and the pose is solved per camera.
  • A joint solve refines all cameras together with each board's placement, so neighbouring cameras agree at the seams.
  • Per camera: Δx Δy Δz, Δyaw Δpitch Δroll and a verdict against your tolerance; the measured poses become an as-built variant.
  • Unit lens calibration from 10–20 board views (cv::fisheye), or import calibrations from OpenCV, ROS camera_info, Kalibr or JSON.

Live rig health

Every stream, its health, and whether a camera moved

Calibrate ▸ Live rig reads every camera's stream at once and says, per camera, whether it is delivering what the recording needs: frame rate against the module's, interval jitter, dropped frames, exposure and clipping, sharpness and frozen images, and across the rig how far apart the cameras' frames arrive.

  • Drift indicator: the planned body outline and horizon drawn over the live image through the lens model. If the body edge sits elsewhere, the camera has probably been knocked; the installation check then measures by how much.
  • Save frames or hand them straight to the installation check.
  • Before each run: vantage rig.vantage --rig-health 10 health.json exits with 3 if a camera is faulty.
  • Stream passwords are kept in a private file in your settings folder, never in the project.

Capture times are when frames reached Vantage, not sensor timestamps, so network and encoder delays are included. The recording uses synthetic streams rendered from the plan.

AI assistant

Ask the design questions. Get answers from the analysis.

The assistant runs on a model hosted on Groq with your own API key. It reads the design only through Vantage's own tools, so every number in an answer comes from the analysis, and it can only propose changes.

From a question to a measured proposal

  • Answers with reasons: why ground is blind (field of view, image frame, body occlusion), which camera matters most, what the studies show.
  • Agent mode: give it a goal; it plans a checklist, runs studies, searches camera poses, and proposes the best change with its effect.
  • Proposals show the change and the predicted effect; apply them as one undo step or as a new variant, and the card shows the measured result.
  • A project brief with your requirements goes with every question; slash commands such as /optimise QR and /compare.

Only camera poses, lens and module names and analysis figures are sent to Groq, never the vehicle model, images or files; every lookup is listed with what it sent. In this recording the model's replies were scripted; the lookups, the optimisation and the proposal were computed live by Vantage.

Export

Hand the design to the people and machines that need it.

Twenty-eight formats, each with a live preview. Units, angles and the coordinate frame are chosen per export, and every file says which convention it uses.

Datasets

Calibration in your dataset's format

The calibration your data pipeline reads, written straight from the design or from the measured as-built poses, in the vehicle frame and origin you choose.

  • nuScenes sensor.json and calibrated_sensor.json: cameras, lidars and radars to ego, quaternions w first.
  • KITTI calib_cam_to_cam.txt, every sensor's pose in the vehicle frame, and calib_velo_to_cam.txt with a lidar.
  • ROS camera_info per camera, and a Kalibr camchain that Vantage also reads back.
  • Fisheye lenses are written as Kannala–Brandt (equidistant), and each file says how its format's tools treat that.

Download

Install Vantage.

Bundles that need no Python are built by the repository's Build installers workflow and attached to a release whenever a version is tagged. Until the first version is tagged, install from source with Python 3.10 or later, or build a bundle for your machine.

Linux

Bundle, no Python

tar xf Vantage-*-linux-x86_64.tar.gz
cd Vantage-*-linux-x86_64
./install.sh
vantage --self-test

Installs for your user with a menu entry and the .vantage file type.

Windows

Installer

Vantage-<version>-windows-x64-setup.exe

Per-user install without administrator rights, a Start menu entry, and vantage-cli.exe for batch mode.

macOS

App

unzip Vantage-*-macos-arm64.zip
mv Vantage.app /Applications

Not signed yet: the first time, right-click the app and choose Open.

Python

From source

git clone https://github.com/Sherin-SEF-AI/Vantage
cd Vantage
python3 -m pip install .
vantage --sample

Add [step] for STEP import and [keyring] to keep the assistant's key in the system keyring.
